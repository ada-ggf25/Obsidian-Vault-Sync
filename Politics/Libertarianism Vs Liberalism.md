People often mix these up because **“liberalism” is a broad family** of ideas, while **libertarianism is a narrower, more extreme member of that family (in many versions).**

## Liberalism (broad tradition)

Core idea: **protect individual rights and freedoms** through **rule of law**, **constitutional government**, and **political equality** (e.g., free speech, religious freedom, due process, voting rights).

But liberalism splits into major strands:

- **Classical liberalism**:  
    Emphasises **limited government**, **free markets**, strong property rights, and “negative liberty” (freedom _from_ interference).
    
- **Modern / social liberalism**:  
    Still pro–civil liberties and democracy, but thinks government should **actively secure fair opportunity and basic welfare** (e.g., public education, social safety net, some regulation, anti-discrimination laws). This is often linked to **center-left** politics in the US.
    

## Libertarianism (narrower, more maximalist)

Core idea: **individual liberty is the overriding political value**, especially **freedom from coercion**, with very strong **property rights**.

Typical libertarian view:

- The state should be **minimal** (“night-watchman state”: police, courts, national defense) **or even zero** (anarcho-capitalism).
    
- **Low taxes**, minimal regulation, minimal welfare/redistribution.
    
- Strong support for civil liberties (speech, privacy, bodily autonomy), but _less_ support for using the state to pursue equality or social outcomes.
    

## The clearest differences (in practice)

**1) Role of the state**

- **Liberalism:** State is legitimate not only to protect rights, but (in modern liberalism) also to **promote fair opportunity and protect people from severe deprivation**.
    
- **Libertarianism:** State is justified mainly to **prevent force/fraud**; many other interventions are seen as unjust coercion.
    

**2) Equality and redistribution**

- **Liberalism (modern):** Often supports **some redistribution** (tax-funded services, welfare) to ensure meaningful opportunity.
    
- **Libertarianism:** Generally opposes redistribution as a violation of property rights (taxation beyond minimal functions is often viewed as coercive).
    

**3) Regulation**

- **Liberalism (modern):** More open to regulation (labor, environment, consumer protection) to prevent harms and power imbalances.
    
- **Libertarianism:** More skeptical; prefers contracts/markets and sees many regulations as rights-violations or inefficient.
    

**4) Shared ground**  
They often agree on:

- Free speech, freedom of religion
    
- Due process, limits on arbitrary state power
    
- Tolerance/pluralism (though they justify it differently)
    

## Quick rule of thumb

- **Liberalism:** “Freedom + rights + democratic institutions” (and for many liberals, also “+ a safety net and fair opportunity”).
    
- **Libertarianism:** “Maximise individual freedom from coercion, minimise the state.”

### In political “left–right” terms (roughly: size/role of the state in the economy)

- **Classical liberalism** is usually **centre-right / right** on economics (free markets, low regulation, limited state).
    
- **Libertarianism** is usually **further right on economics** (even less state, lower taxes, less regulation).
    

So on _economic policy_, your intuition is mostly right: **libertarianism tends to be “more right” than classical liberalism**.

### But “far-right” is a different label

“Far-right” usually implies things like **nationalism/nativism**, **authoritarianism**, **strong social hierarchy**, sometimes **anti-democratic** tendencies.

Most libertarians are almost the opposite on those axes:

- typically **anti-authoritarian**
    
- often **pro free speech**
    
- often **pro immigration / open borders** (at least in principle)
    
- often **socially permissive** (drug legalization, privacy, etc.)
    

So calling libertarianism “far-right” can be misleading because it’s **economically radical** but **not usually authoritarian or nationalist**.

### The better picture: 2 axes

Think of politics as:

1. **Economic axis** (market vs state intervention)
    
2. **Liberty vs authority** (civil liberties vs coercive control)
    

- **Libertarians:** market-leaning **and** strongly liberty-leaning
    
- **Classical liberals:** market-leaning, liberty-leaning (usually less absolutist than libertarians)
    
- **Conservatives (many):** market-leaning **but** more authority/tradition-leaning
    
- **Modern liberals/social democrats:** more state intervention, liberty-leaning (varies)
    

### Europe vs US language trap

In **Europe**, “liberal” often means **pro-market** (closer to classical liberal).  
In the **US**, “liberal” often means **modern/social liberal** (centre-left).

So: **libertarianism is usually “more right” economically than classical liberalism**, but **not necessarily “far-right” overall**.
