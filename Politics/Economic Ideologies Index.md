Here’s a **broad, “as comprehensive as practical”** list of **economic ideologies** (and closely related traditions). There’s no single official taxonomy, so I’m grouping them by the _big questions_ they answer: **who owns**, **how decisions are coordinated**, and **what the state should do**.

## Capitalist and pro-market families

**Private ownership + markets are central** (state role varies).

- **Laissez-faire capitalism** — minimal regulation/tax; markets do most coordination.
    
- **Classical liberalism** — markets + property rights + limited state (rule of law).
    
- **Neoliberalism** — market-oriented reforms (trade liberalization, deregulation, privatization; often with independent central banks).
    
- **Libertarian capitalism / minarchism** — “night-watchman” state; strong property rights.
    
- **Anarcho-capitalism** — no state; law/security provided by private institutions.
    
- **Chicago-school market liberalism** — strong faith in markets; monetarist leaning in macro.
    
- **Austrian economics–inspired libertarianism** — anti-central planning; emphasis on entrepreneurship and price signals.
    
- **Supply-side economics** — emphasize incentives, lower marginal taxes, deregulation.
    
- **Stakeholder capitalism** — firms should serve stakeholders (workers/customers/community), not only shareholders.
    
- **Welfare capitalism** — capitalism plus robust social insurance/welfare provisions.
    
- **Conservative market economy** — pro-market with emphasis on stability, tradition, and social order (varies by country).
    
- **Free-trade liberalism** — specialization and open trade as key to prosperity.
    
- **Protectionist capitalism** — capitalism but with tariffs/industrial protection.
    
- **Economic liberalism (European sense)** — generally pro-market, pro-competition (often “center-right”).
    

## Mixed economies and reformist traditions

**Markets exist, but the state actively shapes outcomes**.

- **Social liberalism (modern liberalism)** — civil liberties + regulation + welfare to ensure fair opportunity.
    
- **Keynesianism (policy ideology in practice)** — active fiscal policy to stabilize demand and employment.
    
- **Neokeynesian / New Keynesian policy frameworks** — stabilization with monetary policy + some fiscal role.
    
- **Social democracy** — regulated capitalism + strong unions + welfare state + redistribution.
    
- **Third Way** — blend of market mechanisms with social investment and targeted welfare.
    
- **Social market economy (Soziale Marktwirtschaft)** — competitive markets + strong social protections (often linked to Germany).
    
- **Ordoliberalism** — strong state to enforce competition rules and prevent monopolies/capture, but not to run the economy.
    
- **Dirigisme** — state “steers” industrial structure via planning, champions, credit guidance (common label for postwar France).
    
- **Developmental statism / developmentalism** — export/industrial policy, strategic sectors, state coordination (East Asian “developmental state” archetype).
    
- **Industrial policy nationalism** — strategic support for domestic industries for security/sovereignty.
    
- **Corporatism (neo-corporatism)** — wage/price/social pacts coordinated among state, business, and labor unions.
    
- **Christian democracy economic tradition** — “social market” style: markets + solidarity/subsidiarity (varies).
    
- **Institutionalism (policy tradition)** — focus on institutions, bargaining power, legal structures shaping markets.
    

## State-centered capitalist variants

**Capitalism exists, but the state (or a ruling elite) dominates key levers**.

- **State capitalism** — state owns or controls major firms/finance while using markets in other areas.
    
- **National capitalism** — markets + strong state role to advance national goals.
    
- **Military–industrial developmentalism** — security-driven industrial buildup (historically common).
    
- **Crony capitalism (degenerate form, not an “ideal”)** — markets distorted by favoritism, corruption, capture.
    

## Socialist and anti-capitalist families

**Limit/replace private ownership of productive capital; democratize or collectivize control**.

### Democratic / libertarian / bottom-up socialism

- **Democratic socialism** — socialism via democratic politics; often pluralist, pro-civil liberties.
    
- **Market socialism** — social/worker ownership with market pricing and competition.
    
- **Liberal socialism** — tries to reconcile liberal rights with social ownership/strong equality (family of views).
    
- **Libertarian socialism** — anti-capitalist + anti-authoritarian; decentralization and self-management.
    
- **Syndicalism** — worker unions as core economic decision units.
    
- **Anarcho-syndicalism** — syndicalism + anarchist anti-state stance.
    
- **Anarcho-communism** — stateless, classless, common ownership; distribution by need (varies).
    
- **Guild socialism** — industries run by guild-like worker organizations.
    
- **Council communism** — worker councils, anti-party centralism.
    
- **Participatory economics (Parecon)** — worker/consumer councils + participatory planning (anti-market, anti-central state).
    

### Marxist / revolutionary / party-led socialism

- **Marxism (broad)** — class analysis; critique of capitalism; many branches.
    
- **Marxism–Leninism** — one-party state, central planning, “vanguard” strategy.
    
- **Trotskyism** — permanent revolution; critique of bureaucratic “degenerated workers’ states.”
    
- **Maoism** — peasant-based revolutionary strategy; mass line; party-led development.
    
- **Titoism / Yugoslav self-management socialism** — historic model emphasizing worker self-management (with party context).
    
- **Market reforms within one-party socialism** — hybrid “socialist market economy” approaches (varies by country).
    

## Alternative ownership traditions (neither standard capitalism nor standard socialism)

- **Distributism** — widely distributed small property ownership; anti-monopoly, pro-family/community.
    
- **Georgism (Geoism)** — land value tax; treat land rent as common resource while keeping markets.
    
- **Mutualism** — markets + possession-based property norms; anti-capitalist but not necessarily anti-market.
    
- **Cooperative economics / cooperativism** — economy centered on co-ops, mutuals, credit unions.
    
- **Communalism / libertarian municipalism** — economy governed by democratic municipalities federated together.
    

## Nationalist/authoritarian political-economy models (historical and present)

(These are “economic ideologies” insofar as they prescribe ownership/coordination, but they’re also political regimes.)

- **Economic nationalism** — prioritize domestic control, strategic autonomy, protectionism.
    
- **Mercantilism (historical)** — state-managed trade surpluses, colonial extraction, bullion focus.
    
- **Autarky** — self-sufficiency; minimize trade dependence.
    
- **Fascist corporatism (historical)** — state-coordinated employer/worker “corporations,” suppress independent labor; mixed ownership under authoritarian control.
    

## Environmental and sustainability-centered economic ideologies

- **Green growth** — decouple growth from emissions via tech, regulation, carbon pricing.
    
- **Steady-state economics** — stable throughput; ecological limits; focus on well-being over GDP growth.
    
- **Degrowth** — intentional downscaling of material/energy use in rich economies; equity and ecology first.
    
- **Eco-socialism** — socialism oriented around ecological constraints and justice.
    
- **Eco-capitalism** — markets + strong environmental pricing/rights frameworks.
    
- **Circular economy (framework/ideology-adjacent)** — design out waste, reuse/repair/recycle; can fit many systems.
    

## Religious/ethical economic traditions

- **Islamic economics** — prohibits interest in many interpretations; risk-sharing finance; zakat; ethical constraints.
    
- **Catholic social teaching economics** — solidarity/subsidiarity; dignity of work; often aligns with “social market” ideas.
    
- **Buddhist economics** — minimize suffering/attachment; right livelihood; sufficiency over maximization.
    
- **Gandhian economics** — local self-reliance, simplicity, small-scale production.
