#philosophy #politics

**Left-libertarianism / libertarian socialism** is the **anti-capitalist, pro-freedom** side of the left.

It says (roughly):  
**“People should be as free as possible — and that includes freedom from bosses and concentrated private economic power — so we should organise the economy democratically without an authoritarian state.”**

## Core ideas

- **Anti-authoritarian:** strongly suspicious of centralized state power (police-state, one-party rule, etc.).
    
- **Anti-capitalist (in most versions):** objects to a system where a small group owns workplaces/land/capital and others must sell labor under unequal bargaining power.
    
- **Worker/self-management:** workplaces should be run by workers (co-ops), or by democratic councils.
    
- **Decentralization:** prefer local control, federations of communes/co-ops, bottom-up decision-making.
    
- **Freedom + equality together:** not “state equality” imposed from above, but **equal power** in the institutions you live and work in.
    

## How it differs from other things

### Versus right-libertarianism (US “libertarian”)

- Right-libertarianism: **max property rights + minimal state** → capitalism is fine, just shrink government.
    
- Libertarian socialism: **max liberty + democratic economy** → capitalism itself can be coercive, so change ownership and control.
    

### Versus Marxism-Leninism / “state socialism”

- Marxism-Leninism: accepts a strong state/party as a path to socialism.
    
- Libertarian socialism: says that path tends to **produce new ruling elites** and crush freedom; wants socialism **without** an authoritarian state.
    

### Versus modern social democracy

- Social democracy: keep capitalism but regulate it + welfare state.
    
- Libertarian socialism: wants to **replace** capitalist ownership with democratic ownership, not just regulate it.
    

## What it looks like in practice

Common proposals:

- **Worker cooperatives** (workers own and vote on management)
    
- **Co-determination** (workers share control; sometimes seen as a “half step”)
    
- **Community/municipal ownership** of essential services
    
- **Unions + workplace democracy**
    
- **Mutual aid networks** and local assemblies
    
- **Decentralized planning** or market socialism (varies by branch)
    

## Major branches / labels you’ll see

- **Anarchism** (esp. social anarchism)
    
- **Anarcho-syndicalism** (unions as the backbone of a democratic economy)
    
- **Council communism** (worker councils, anti-party-centralism)
    
- **Mutualism** (Proudhon; markets can exist but without capitalist ownership of productive property)
    
- **Communalism / libertarian municipalism** (Bookchin; democratic cities/communes federated together)
    

## A simple intuition

- Right-libertarian: **“The state is the main threat to freedom.”**
    
- Libertarian socialist: **“The state _and_ concentrated private power can both threaten freedom.”**
