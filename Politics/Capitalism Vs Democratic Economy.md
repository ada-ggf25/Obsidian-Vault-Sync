A good way to see it is: **capitalism is mainly about _who owns_ and _who controls_ productive assets**, while a “democratic economy” is mainly about **making that ownership/control more democratic** (especially at work), sometimes with different ways of coordinating production.

## Capitalism

**Ownership & control**

- Most businesses are owned by **private owners/investors (shareholders)**.
    
- Decisions are made by **owners and managers they appoint**, not by workers as a group.
    

**How income is distributed**

- Workers get **wages**.
    
- Owners get **profits/dividends/capital gains** (returns to capital).
    

**Coordination**

- Mostly via **markets**: prices, competition, supply/demand.
    
- The state sets rules (property law, contracts, regulation), varies by country.
    

## Democratic economy (umbrella idea)

There isn’t one single model, but the common core is:

**Ownership & control**

- Productive assets are owned/controlled **democratically**—for example by:
    
    - **workers** (worker co-ops),
        
    - **communities/municipalities** (public or community ownership),
        
    - or **mixed stakeholder models** (workers + community + users).
        

**Workplace governance**

- Big decisions (strategy, leadership, profit use) are made **one person–one vote** or similar democratic mechanisms, rather than “one share–one vote.”
    

**Distribution**

- Surplus is allocated by democratic rules: e.g. reinvestment, higher pay, social funds, dividends to worker-owners, community benefits.
    

**Coordination**

- Can still use **markets** (market socialism / cooperative markets), or
    
- Use **planning** (state planning or decentralized planning), or
    
- A hybrid.
    

## The key differences in one line each

- **Who has power at work?**  
    Capitalism: owners/shareholders.  
    Democratic economy: workers/stakeholders collectively.
    
- **What does “ownership” mean?**  
    Capitalism: tradable private property (shares).  
    Democratic economy: ownership tied to membership/citizenship/participation (often not freely tradable).
    
- **What’s the main goal?**  
    Capitalism: typically maximize profit/returns (varies by firm).  
    Democratic economy: prioritize democratic goals—good jobs, resilience, fairness, community benefit—profit can matter but isn’t the sole boss.
    

## Concrete example: the same company under two systems

**A supermarket chain**

- Capitalist version: shareholders elect board → board hires CEO → CEO sets strategy; workers can negotiate via unions but don’t run the firm.
    
- Worker-co-op version: workers elect leadership/board; major choices voted on; profits shared or reinvested by vote.
    

## Important nuance

Many real economies are **mixed**:

- Capitalist countries can have **co-ops** (Mondragon in Spain is a famous example) and **public utilities**.
    
- “Democratic economy” proposals often don’t mean “no markets,” just **more democracy in ownership and decision-making**.
